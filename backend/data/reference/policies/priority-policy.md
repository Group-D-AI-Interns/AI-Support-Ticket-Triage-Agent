## 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to establish a standardized approach for classifying, prioritizing, and managing incidents, service requests, problems, and operational issues across the organization.

This policy ensures that business-critical issues receive appropriate attention based on their impact and urgency, while ensuring resources are allocated efficiently.

## 2. SCOPE

This policy applies to:

- IT Service Desk personnel.
- IT Support Analysts.
- Technical Support Teams.
- Infrastructure and Network Teams.
- Application Support Teams.
- Service Delivery Managers.
- Business Users.
- Third-party service providers.

All incidents and service requests recorded in the organization's service management platform must follow this policy.

## 3. PRIORITY CLASSIFICATION

Priority is determined using two primary factors:

**Impact:** The extent to which the issue affects business operations, users, systems, or services.

**Urgency:** The amount of time available before the issue causes significant business disruption.

Priority is calculated using the combination of impact and urgency.

## 4. PRIORITY LEVELS

### P1 – CRITICAL

**Definition:** A major business disruption affecting critical services, systems, or a significant number of users.

Examples:
- Complete network outage.
- Enterprise-wide application failure.
- Production database unavailable.
- Cybersecurity incident affecting critical infrastructure.
- Business-critical system unavailable across multiple departments.

**Required Action:**
- Immediate investigation.
- Immediate escalation to technical management.
- Incident manager assigned.
- Major Incident Management process initiated.
- Continuous communication with affected stakeholders.

### P2 – HIGH

**Definition:** A significant service disruption affecting multiple users, departments, or important business functions.

Examples:
- Department-wide application outage.
- Critical business functionality unavailable.
- Significant network degradation.
- Multiple users unable to access essential business systems.

**Required Action:**
- High-priority investigation.
- Assignment to the appropriate technical team.
- Escalation if progress is delayed.
- Regular updates to affected users.

### P3 – MEDIUM

**Definition:** An issue affecting an individual user or a limited number of users where business operations can continue using alternative methods.

Examples:
- Individual user unable to access an application.
- Email functionality partially affected.
- Printer unavailable for a department.
- Non-critical system functionality experiencing errors.

**Required Action:**
- Standard investigation.
- Assignment to the relevant support team.
- Resolution within the agreed SLA.
- Updates provided when necessary.

### P4 – LOW

**Definition:** Minor issues, general requests, or problems with minimal business impact.

Examples:
- Software installation requests.
- General access requests.
- Minor configuration changes.
- Non-urgent information requests.
- Cosmetic application issues.

**Required Action:**
- Process through the normal service queue.
- Schedule according to available resources.
- Complete within the applicable SLA.

## 5. IMPACT CLASSIFICATION

| Impact | Description |
|---|---|
| High | Critical business operations or multiple departments affected. |
| Medium | A department or several users affected. |
| Low | One user or a minor business function affected. |

## 6. URGENCY CLASSIFICATION

| Urgency | Description |
|---|---|
| High | Immediate attention required to prevent significant business disruption. |
| Medium | Issue requires timely attention but temporary workarounds exist. |
| Low | Issue can be addressed through normal operational scheduling. |

## 7. PRIORITY MATRIX

The following matrix determines the initial priority.

| Impact / Urgency | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | P1 – Critical | P2 – High | P3 – Medium |
| Medium | P2 – High | P3 – Medium | P4 – Low |
| Low | P3 – Medium | P4 – Low | P4 – Low |

## 8. PRIORITY OVERRIDES

The Service Desk Manager or Incident Manager may override the calculated priority when justified by business circumstances.

Examples include:

- Regulatory or legal compliance exposure.
- Security incidents.
- Financially significant business disruption.
- Critical executive or customer-facing services.
- Imminent business deadlines.

All priority overrides must include:
1. Reason for the override.
2. Name of the person authorizing the change.
3. Date and time of the change.
4. Supporting business justification.

## 9. PRIORITY REVIEW

Priority must be reviewed when:

- The number of affected users changes.
- Business impact increases or decreases.
- A workaround becomes available.
- An incident escalates.
- Additional technical information becomes available.

The assigned support team must ensure that the priority accurately reflects the current business impact.

## 10. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

**Service Desk Analyst**
- Record and classify incoming incidents.
- Determine initial impact and urgency.
- Assign the appropriate priority.
- Escalate incidents when necessary.

**Technical Support Team**
- Investigate assigned incidents.
- Provide technical updates.
- Request priority reviews where necessary.

**Incident Manager**
- Coordinate major incidents.
- Approve justified priority overrides.
- Ensure escalation procedures are followed.

**Service Delivery Manager**
- Monitor priority distribution.
- Review policy compliance.
- Identify recurring operational issues.

## 11. COMPLIANCE

All employees and support teams must comply with this policy.

Failure to follow priority classification procedures may result in inaccurate reporting, delayed incident resolution, and failure to meet organizational service commitments.

## 12. POLICY REVIEW AND APPROVAL

This policy must be reviewed annually or whenever significant operational changes occur.
