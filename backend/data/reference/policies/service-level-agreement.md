## 1. PURPOSE

The purpose of this policy is to define the service level commitments, response targets, resolution targets, escalation procedures, and client classification standards applicable to services provided by the organization.

This policy establishes a structured service delivery framework that recognizes different contractual client categories while ensuring fair, consistent, and measurable service delivery.

## 2. SCOPE

This policy applies to:

- Internal IT support services.
- Service Desk operations.
- Infrastructure support.
- Network support.
- Application support.
- End-user computing.
- IT service providers and contractors.
- All registered corporate clients and customers.

All incidents and service requests must be processed according to the applicable client category and incident priority.

## 3. SLA DEFINITIONS

**Service Level Agreement (SLA):** A formal agreement defining the expected level of service between a service provider and its customer.

**Client Classification:** The categorization of clients according to their contractual value, service agreement, business importance, and agreed support entitlement.

**Response Time:** The time taken for the support team to acknowledge an incident and begin the appropriate initial assessment.

**Resolution Time:** The time taken to restore the affected service or provide an agreed resolution.

**Escalation:** The process of transferring an issue to a higher technical or management level.

**Business Hours:** The organization's officially defined operational working hours.

**Service Availability:** The percentage of time a service is operational and accessible.

## 4. CLIENT CLASSIFICATION FRAMEWORK

The organization shall implement a five-tier client classification structure.

Each client must be assigned a classification based on their contractual agreement, commercial relationship, business importance, and service entitlement.

### 4.1 Client Categories

| Category | Classification | Service Entitlement |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond | Strategic Enterprise | Highest service entitlement |
| Platinum | Premium Enterprise | Enhanced service entitlement |
| Gold | Preferred Corporate | Priority service entitlement |
| Silver | Standard Corporate | Standard service entitlement |
| Bronze | Basic Corporate | Standard contracted support |

### 4.2 DIAMOND CLIENTS

**Classification:** Strategic Enterprise Clients

Diamond clients receive the highest contractual service entitlement.

Typical characteristics:
- Major enterprise customers.
- Strategic business partners.
- High-value contractual relationships.
- Clients with business-critical operations.
- Clients with dedicated support agreements.

Service entitlements:
- Highest priority within comparable incident classifications.
- Dedicated account or service management.
- 24/7 critical incident support, where contracted.
- Dedicated escalation channels.
- Proactive service monitoring, where contracted.
- Frequent incident progress updates.
- Priority access to specialist technical resources.

### 4.3 PLATINUM CLIENTS

**Classification:** Premium Enterprise Clients

Platinum clients receive enhanced service support and expedited escalation.

Typical characteristics:
- Large corporate organizations.
- High-value customers.
- Clients with premium support contracts.
- Clients with multiple operational dependencies.

Service entitlements:
- Enhanced incident response targets.
- Priority technical escalation.
- Extended support hours, where contracted.
- Dedicated service delivery contact.
- Regular service performance reporting.
- Expedited handling of business-critical incidents.

### 4.4 GOLD CLIENTS

**Classification:** Preferred Corporate Clients

Gold clients receive enhanced support compared with standard corporate customers.

Typical characteristics:
- Established corporate customers.
- Medium-to-large business accounts.
- Clients with enhanced service agreements.
- Customers requiring dependable operational support.

Service entitlements:
- Priority allocation over standard-tier tickets of comparable impact and urgency.
- Standard business-hours support.
- Defined escalation channels.
- Regular incident progress updates.
- Monthly or quarterly service reporting, where contracted.

### 4.5 SILVER CLIENTS

**Classification:** Standard Corporate Clients

Silver clients receive standard corporate support services.

Typical characteristics:
- Small and medium-sized businesses.
- Standard contracted customers.
- Clients with limited support requirements.

Service entitlements:
- Standard incident management.
- Standard SLA response targets.
- Access to the Service Desk.
- Normal technical escalation procedures.
- Progress updates according to incident priority.

### 4.6 BRONZE CLIENTS

**Classification:** Basic Corporate Clients

Bronze clients receive the organization's basic contracted support services.

Typical characteristics:
- Entry-level service customers.
- Clients with basic support agreements.
- Customers with limited service requirements.

Service entitlements:
- Standard ticket registration.
- Normal support queue allocation.
- Basic technical troubleshooting.
- Contractually agreed response targets.
- Standard escalation procedures.

Bronze clients remain entitled to appropriate emergency handling when an incident presents a critical business or operational risk.

## 5. CLIENT CLASSIFICATION ELIGIBILITY AND GOVERNANCE

Client categories must be assigned and maintained by the Account Management or Commercial Management Department in consultation with Service Delivery Management.

Classification may be determined using:

- Annual contract value.
- Contracted service package.
- Business criticality.
- Number of supported users or locations.
- Service availability requirements.
- Dedicated support arrangements.
- Strategic partnership agreements.

The organization must maintain an official client classification register.

Each client record must contain:

1. Client name.
2. Client account number.
3. Assigned classification.
4. Contract reference.
5. Effective classification date.
6. Contract renewal date.
7. Approved service entitlement.
8. Account manager.
9. SLA owner.

Client classifications must not be changed by individual Service Desk analysts without authorization.

## 6. INCIDENT PRIORITY AND CLIENT CLASSIFICATION

The organization must distinguish between incident priority and client classification.

**Incident priority** determines the seriousness of an incident.

**Client classification** determines the service entitlement applicable to the customer.

The two classifications must work together when determining the applicable SLA.

For example:

- A Diamond client's P1 incident receives the Diamond P1 SLA.
- A Gold client's P2 incident receives the Gold P2 SLA.
- A Bronze client's P1 incident must still receive critical incident treatment.

Client classification must never be used to downgrade a critical incident affecting another customer.

## 9. CLIENT-BASED ESCALATION PROCEDURE

Escalation must consider both incident priority and client classification.

### Diamond Clients
- Immediate notification to the Incident Manager for P1 incidents.
- Direct escalation to senior technical management.
- Dedicated service delivery communication.
- Executive stakeholder updates where necessary.

### Platinum Clients
- Expedited escalation to technical team leaders.
- Service Delivery Manager notification for P1 incidents.
- Regular progress reporting.

### Gold Clients
- Escalation through standard technical management channels.
- Service Delivery Manager notification for significant SLA risks.

### Silver Clients
- Standard technical escalation procedures.
- Escalation to the Service Desk Manager where SLA targets are at risk.

### Bronze Clients
- Normal support queue escalation.
- Escalation according to incident severity and contractual requirements.

Regardless of category, all P1 incidents must be handled under the organization's major incident management process.

## 10. SLA MEASUREMENT RULES

### 10.1 Response Time

Response time begins when a valid incident or service request is successfully logged in the service management platform.

Response is considered achieved when:

- The ticket has been acknowledged.
- An initial assessment has been performed.
- The ticket has been assigned to the appropriate support team or owner.

An automated ticket receipt alone does not constitute a meaningful technical response.

### 10.2 Resolution Time

Resolution time begins when the ticket is logged and accepted into the applicable SLA measurement process.

Resolution is achieved when:

- The service has been restored; or
- The agreed solution has been implemented; and
- The resolution has been documented.

Where applicable, user confirmation must be obtained before final closure.

### 10.3 SLA Timer Application

The service management platform must automatically determine the applicable SLA based on:

1. Client account identification.
2. Registered client classification.
3. Incident priority.
4. Applicable service agreement.
5. Support calendar.
6. Approved SLA exclusions.

Manual changes must be documented and authorized.

## 11. SLA BREACH MANAGEMENT

An SLA breach occurs when the agreed response or resolution target is exceeded without an approved exclusion or adjustment.

When an SLA breach occurs, the responsible team must:

1. Document the reason for the breach.
2. Record the actual response or resolution time.
3. Identify the responsible support team.
4. Document operational or technical dependencies.
5. Implement corrective actions.
6. Escalate recurring breaches to management.
7. Notify the relevant account manager where required.

Repeated breaches involving Diamond and Platinum clients must be reviewed by senior service management.

## 12. SLA PAUSE CONDITIONS

SLA timers may only be paused when an approved pause condition applies.

Examples include:

- Waiting for information from the requesting client.
- Waiting for approved customer authorization.
- Waiting for a scheduled maintenance window.
- Waiting for an agreed third-party dependency where the contract permits suspension.

The following requirements apply:

- The pause reason must be recorded.
- The date and time must be documented.
- The responsible party must be identified.
- The timer must resume when the blocking condition is removed.

Internal technical delays, insufficient staffing, or failure to assign a ticket are not automatically valid reasons for pausing an SLA.

## 13. SERVICE LEVEL PERFORMANCE REPORTING

The IT Service Management team must produce regular SLA performance reports.

Reports should include:

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| SLA Compliance | Percentage of tickets meeting SLA targets. |
| SLA Breaches | Number of tickets exceeding SLA targets. |
| First Response Time | Average time taken to acknowledge incidents. |
| Resolution Time | Average time taken to resolve incidents. |
| First Contact Resolution | Percentage of incidents resolved at first contact. |
| Open Tickets | Number of unresolved incidents. |
| Backlog | Number of outstanding tickets. |
| Reopened Tickets | Number of previously resolved tickets reopened. |
| Client Tier Performance | SLA performance by client category. |
| Client Satisfaction | Customer satisfaction by client category. |

### 13.1 SLA Compliance Calculation

SLA compliance is calculated as:

SLA Compliance (%) = (Tickets meeting SLA / Total eligible tickets) × 100

Example:

If 950 out of 1,000 eligible tickets meet their SLA:

SLA Compliance = 95%

Reports must allow management to compare performance across Diamond, Platinum, Gold, Silver, and Bronze clients.

## 14. SLA PERFORMANCE TARGETS

The following are proposed internal performance targets:

| KPI | Target |
|---|---:|
| Overall SLA Compliance | ≥ 95% |
| Diamond Client SLA Compliance | ≥ 98% |
| Platinum Client SLA Compliance | ≥ 97% |
| Gold Client SLA Compliance | ≥ 95% |
| Silver Client SLA Compliance | ≥ 93% |
| Bronze Client SLA Compliance | ≥ 90% |
| Ticket Documentation Compliance | ≥ 98% |
| First Contact Resolution | ≥ 70% |

These are internal performance objectives, not substitutes for contractual SLA commitments.

Performance must be reviewed monthly.

## 15. THIRD-PARTY SERVICE PROVIDERS

Where services are supported by external vendors, the organization must ensure that:

- Vendor SLAs are documented.
- Escalation contacts are maintained.
- Vendor response commitments are understood.
- Dependencies are reflected in service delivery agreements.
- Vendor performance is reviewed regularly.

Internal customer-facing SLAs must account for third-party dependencies and must not promise commitments that the organization cannot reasonably deliver.

## 16. CLIENT CLASSIFICATION REVIEW

Client classifications must be reviewed:

- During annual contract renewals.
- When contractual service levels change.
- When annual contract value changes significantly.
- When the client's operational requirements change.
- When approved by Commercial Management.
- When a strategic partnership is established or terminated.

Any upgrade or downgrade must be formally authorized and recorded.

Changes must include an effective date and must not retrospectively alter existing SLA measurements.

## 17. EXCLUSIONS

The following situations may be excluded from SLA calculations where explicitly permitted by the applicable service agreement:

- Approved maintenance windows.
- Events outside the agreed service scope.
- Force majeure events.
- User-requested postponements.
- Approved waiting periods under the documented pause rules.

All exclusions must be recorded and supported by evidence.

## 18. GOVERNANCE AND POLICY COMPLIANCE

The IT Service Delivery Manager is responsible for ensuring that:

- SLA targets are monitored.
- Client classifications are correctly applied.
- SLA breaches are investigated.
- Performance reports are distributed.
- Corrective actions are implemented.
- Service improvement initiatives are tracked.

The Commercial or Account Management Department is responsible for maintaining approved client classifications and associated contractual entitlements.

All service teams must comply with this policy.

This policy must be reviewed annually or whenever significant changes occur to service delivery arrangements or commercial agreements.
